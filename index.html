How to Compare Inflatable Boat Quotes Fairly: A Worksheet Approach

Two inflatable boat quotations rarely describe the same thing. One includes an air floor, a pump and paddles; another quotes a bare hull with a lower figure and a list of options. One quotes delivery to a port; another to a factory gate. Comparing the totals directly rewards whichever offer leaves out the most. Raft Inflatable, the export catalog of Weihai Reachsea Outdoors Co., Ltd., publishes a quote comparison worksheet guide to avoid that trap. This article explains how to use it.

Supplier quote comparison worksheet for inflatable boats. Image: raftinflatable.com.

Normalize the product before the price

The worksheet starts by aligning what is being quoted. Create one row for the selected model and separate lines for floors, accessories and options. Record unanswered specification questions instead of assuming.

The guide's reasoning is simple: comparing totals before the package is aligned can reward an offer that simply excludes more items. Its FAQ defines comparability. What makes two quotes comparable? The same configuration, quantity, packing and delivery assumptions, with differences and exclusions explicitly recorded.

Watch for images that promise more than the text

The worksheet flags a subtle problem: items shown in product images but missing from the written scope. A photo of a boat with a console, seat and bimini does not mean those parts are in the price. Only the written scope counts.

The six worksheet rows

The guide lists the rows to fill for each offer and what to flag if information is missing:

Item Record Flag if
Configuration model, dimensions, construction, floor, option revision unspecified or different
Accessories included items, quantities, exclusions shown in images but absent from scope
Quantity order quantity, MOQ, quantity basis per price unclear minimums or mixed tiers
Packing units per package, dimensions, gross weight missing or estimated
Delivery term and place term, named place, transport stages unnamed place or unclear duty and freight
Evidence document title, model scope, revision or date unsupported claims

The FAQ answers a tempting shortcut. Should uncertain values be entered as zero? No. Mark them as unconfirmed so missing information cannot create a misleadingly low total.

Separate one-time and recurring costs

Some charges happen once: samples, artwork, tooling or other stated setup items. Others repeat with every unit. The guide asks buyers to list setup charges separately from production items, and to ask how quantity changes affect the offer.

It also warns against assuming that a lower unit figure offsets every additional cost or commitment. A supplier with a lower unit price but a large setup charge may cost more for a small first order. Raft Inflatable's MOQ and sample questions guide covers how to ask about these points before the quote arrives.

Delivery terms change the real total

Add columns for packing data, delivery rule and place, lead-time assumptions and required documents. The site's delivery terms comparison guide explains why a named place matters: two quotes on different delivery terms leave different freight, insurance and duty stages to the buyer. Mark estimates clearly rather than letting them look like confirmed figures.

Decide with exceptions visible

The worksheet does not end with a single number. The guide recommends summarizing remaining risks and questions alongside price, keeping the accepted quote revision, and explaining any approved exception.

It is honest about its limits: the worksheet supports a decision, but does not replace technical review, supplier verification or the written agreement.

A worked example

A buyer receives three offers for a 3.6 m aluminum-hull RIB. Offer A includes an air floor, pump and paddles, delivered to a named port. Offer B quotes the hull only, with a lower figure, on a factory-gate basis. Offer C includes accessories but gives estimated packing data and no document list. Filled into the worksheet, Offer B's apparent advantage shrinks once the missing accessories and the transport stages are added as open items. Offer C stays in the running, but with two flags to resolve. The decision is now about known differences, not about which number is smallest.

What the site does not publish

Raft Inflatable does not publish prices, MOQ, lead times, warranty terms or certifications. Every value in the worksheet should come from the written quotation for the selected configuration. The site lists the confirmation points each quote should cover: materials, motor and payload, console, floor and accessory scope, MOQ, packing and destination port, and inspection and document requirements.

Procurement checklist

  • Align the exact configuration before comparing totals.
  • Separate setup, product and delivery costs.
  • Mark unknown values as unconfirmed, never zero.
  • Keep open questions visible in the decision.

One last habit makes the worksheet more useful over time: keep completed worksheets after the order. When the next purchase comes round, the earlier rows show which suppliers answered questions fully, which flags were resolved and how, and which assumptions turned out to be wrong.
